# v0.2.0-alpha.49 — What Stays on Screen Is Yours

**Date:** 2026-10-14

## Highlights

- **A compaction result stays until you are done with it.** It used to fade away on its own after a few seconds, often before you had finished reading it. Now it waits, carries a **Dismiss** button, and clears by itself when you send your next turn in that Journey.
- **"Hide Other N"** on any conversation puts every other one in that Journey into the hidden section and keeps the one you are in. Each hidden conversation still has its own **Reveal**, so undoing it is precise rather than all-or-nothing.
- **"Desktop only"** hides Mirror history from the conversation list, across every Journey, and survives a restart. The control says how many it removed and pressing it again brings them back.
- **This was measured before it was built.** Three Journeys carry 41, 45 and 57 Mirror history entries against 4 Desktop conversations, and 77 of those 143 entries have no title and no messages. The noise is real and it is everywhere, which is what decided the shape of the fix.
- **Nothing is deleted by any of this.** Every control here changes what the list shows, not what exists.
- **All three were verified in the field**, and the field found a layout defect that no test could have.

## Where We Started

Three separate reports, all about the same thing from different directions: the screen was deciding things that should have been yours to decide.

The compaction notice was the clearest. Compacting a conversation is a deliberate act with a result worth reading — what was summarised, what was kept. That result appeared and then faded on a timer. The timer was not an oversight; it was put there on purpose in an earlier release, to stop a stale notice from sitting around after it stopped being true. It solved that, and in solving it it also threw away a result the moment you looked away.

The conversation list was the other two. A Journey's list mixes conversations created in the app with the history of every Mirror session that Journey ever had. Those are different things, and one of them was dominating. **The list shows six entries before it has to be expanded, and both kinds compete for those six slots, sorted together by recency** — so a Journey with four Desktop conversations and forty-one Mirror entries would show you mostly the latter.

Here the honest part: the request to separate them described a friction, and the document capturing it admitted that the three kinds of entry had been **read from the code rather than counted in the data**. So they were counted, by running the exact query the app runs, before anything was built.

| Journey | Mirror history entries | untitled *and* empty | Desktop conversations |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mirror-desktop` | 41 | 22 | 4 |
| `alissonvale-com` | 45 | 19 | — |
| `livro-lideranca-soberana` | 57 | 36 | — |

**77 of 143 Mirror entries, 54%, have neither a title nor a single message.** That measurement did more than confirm the friction — it answered a question the request had left open, which the next section explains.

## What Changed

### A compaction result waits for you

A compaction outcome now has three possible lifetimes instead of two, and which one applies depends on what happened:

- **It worked** → the notice stays, with a **Dismiss** button, and clears on its own when you send your next turn in that Journey. A result is only stale once you have moved past it, and sending a turn is exactly that moment.
- **There was nothing to compact** → it still fades, because there is nothing to read.
- **It failed, was interrupted, or is still running** → unchanged. It stays until you dismiss it.

The earlier release's concern was right and is kept: a notice must not survive into a conversation it no longer describes. What changed is the trigger — **your next turn instead of a stopwatch.** The guard that release left behind was narrowed rather than removed, and it is the reason this change was easy to make correctly: it had written down, in advance, that "an unrelated turn succeeding" was the case most likely to be got wrong.

### Two ways for the list to get shorter, each with its own way back

**"Hide Other N"**, on the right-click menu of any conversation, hides every other conversation in that Journey and keeps the one you are in. It is built on the existing single-conversation hide, so the limit on how many can be hidden lives in one place rather than two — and if that limit is reached, the control says how many it could not hide instead of quietly doing less than it offered.

**"Desktop only"** is a toggle below the list that leaves out Mirror history. When it is on, it reads `Desktop only · 41 Mirror hidden`, so the list is never quietly shorter than you expect. Pressing it again restores everything.

The two never borrow each other's controls. The filter reports what the filter removed; the hidden section reports what is hidden; the list's own "Show N more" reports what the cap held back. **Three reductions, three counts, three inverses.**

### The measurement decided whether the filter was per-Journey or global

The request did not say which, and both were the same amount of work — both kinds of preference already live in the same file, so neither needed a new home. With cost equal, the data chose: 41, 45 and 57 entries is noise that is **universal rather than concentrated in one Journey**, and a preference you would otherwise have to set again in every Journey is better set once.

The field confirmed it. The step that mattered was opening a second Journey's list and finding it already filtered — had that been unwelcome, the measurement would have been right about the data and wrong about the product.

### The heading keeps telling the truth

The number beside **Conversations** still counts every conversation that exists, filtered or not. A filter that also edited that number would make a reduction look like a loss. What was removed is reported by the control that removed it.

## Conscious Exclusions

- **The untitled, empty Mirror entries are hidden, not fixed.** 54% of that history has no title and no messages, and whatever produces those entries is on the Mirror side, outside this application. This release lets you stop looking at them. It does not stop them being created, and that is a different piece of work in a different place.
- **The limit on bulk hiding was never reached in practice.** It allows 256 per Journey and the largest list measured is 61, so the path where it reports a shortfall is covered by tests and has never run in real use. It is claimed as tested, not as verified.
- **A genuinely failed compaction surviving your next turn was not provoked.** The three other cases were exercised in the field; this one needs the agent to actually fail rather than decline, so it stays test-covered rather than field-verified.
- **A settlement still moves the entire conversation.** That was the previous release's stated next step and it is still open. It was measured this cycle rather than fixed: cost tracks conversation size almost exactly, at roughly a quarter of a second per megabyte, with the worst single recorded operation taking 41 seconds and the worst complete settlement 113. Nothing about that is user-visible today, because the previous release moved it off the thread that serves your typing — but it is a cost with no brake on it.
- **The turnstile added in the previous release is still a single one for the whole app.** Measured from 816 recorded settlements: two Journeys settling at once happens in 2.9% of cases, never within the same Journey, and it was already happening at the same cost before the turnstile existed. So it is a pre-existing cost made invisible rather than a new one — but "invisible" is the honest word, and the code now says so where someone will find it.

## What We Learned

**Counting the thing before building for it answered a question that taste would only have guessed at.** The filter could have been per-Journey or global. Nothing in the request settled it, both cost the same, and either would have looked defensible in review. Counting the entries settled it in one step, because the shape of the noise — everywhere, not somewhere — is exactly the information the choice needed.

The same discipline cut the other way in the same cycle, which is the part worth keeping. A measurement taken to support reducing what a settlement moves **weakened one of the two arguments for doing it**: contention between Journeys turned out to be rare and pre-existing rather than a new regression. The remaining argument still stands on its own, but the case is smaller than it was before it was checked, and the work was reordered accordingly.

**And the field still sees what tests cannot.** The "Desktop only" control first shipped as a third item in a heading 292 pixels wide that already held a label, a count and a button. Every test passed; it looked crowded immediately. The suggested fix was to reduce it to an icon — ruled out by a rule already written in that same file, that a state must carry text so it survives a greyscale display and a reduced-motion preference rather than resting on colour. So the text stayed and the placement moved to its own row, which also let the count move into the control's label and removed a separate line and a second button. **The final version costs one row where the first cost two.**

## Next Horizon

Reduce what a settlement actually moves, so its cost grows with what a turn added rather than with how long the conversation has become — now with the number that says how much is at stake.

# v0.2.0-alpha.50 — A Sound When It Finishes, and Light Themes You Can Read

**Date:** 2026-10-14

## Highlights

- **A turn announces itself.** When settling finishes, a short tone plays. A turn can take over a minute, and until now the only way to know it had ended was to look.
- **Three tones to choose from**, in Settings, and **selecting one plays it** so you are not picking a sound from a description.
- **A failed settlement sounds different**, and lower, from one that succeeded.
- **A turn you cancelled yourself stays silent** — you already know it happened.
- **Off is genuinely off.** With the switch off, no audio code runs at all, rather than running at zero volume.
- **The Voice tab is now Sound & Voice**, which is where the new settings live.
- **Six controls that were invisible on the light themes are now readable** — the hidden-conversations toggle, both Reveal buttons, the Desktop-only filter, and the two new sound controls.
- **A whole family of colour rules that had never worked now does.** 102 rules referred to colours that were never defined, so they quietly did nothing.

## Where We Started

The request was one sentence: *play a sound when a turn finishes, with a switch in Settings.* Four rounds later it had also uncovered two defects that had nothing to do with sound, which is the more interesting half of this release.

The sound itself was straightforward once one question was answered — *when* does a turn finish? There were three plausible moments and they are seconds apart. The answer was the end of settling, which is the point at which the work is genuinely durable rather than merely visible.

Two properties then came for free, because of how settling already worked. **A cancelled turn cannot make a sound**, because a cancelled turn never starts the bookkeeping that the sound is attached to — nothing had to be written to prevent it. And **one turn cannot announce itself twice**, even though two different places can end a settlement, because the first one to arrive consumes the record and the second finds nothing. Neither needed a guard; both are consequences of the existing shape.

Then the field looked at it, and the rest of this release is what it found.

## What Changed

### The sound, and the choice of it

A tone is a short sequence rather than a single beep — one note can only be moved up or down, which is not enough room for three options you can tell apart and still like.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chime** *(default)* | one short, high note |
| **Rise** | two notes going up |
| **Bell** | two notes going down, with a longer tail |
| *a failure* | one low, longer note — **not a choice** |

The failure tone is deliberately not selectable. Choosing among success sounds is a matter of taste; a failure is not.

Selecting a tone plays it, because choosing a sound you cannot hear is the same problem as judging one from a description — which is exactly what sent the first version back from the field. While the sound is switched off the preview stays silent too, so "off" keeps meaning what it says, and your choice is still remembered for when you switch it back on.

### Why the Voice tab became Sound & Voice

The settings first shipped in the Appearance panel, which was wrong, and the request to move them beside Voice was right. Renaming a tab is a much smaller change than adding one — the tab keeps its internal identity, so keyboard navigation and every other panel are untouched and only the label moves.

### Two light-theme defects that were older than this release

Neither was caused by the sound. The new controls simply stood on top of them and made them visible.

**Colours that were never defined.** Six colour names — the ones for ordinary text, muted text, borders and the accent — were used by 102 rules across the application and declared in none of them. A colour that does not resolve does not fall back to something sensible: the whole instruction is discarded. Text then inherits whatever its container had, which on a light background meant white on white, and borders disappear entirely. Nothing reported this, because stylesheets do not fail loudly. All six are now defined, once for the dark themes and once for the light ones, reusing the light palette that already existed.

**White ink on transparent buttons.** Separately, the light themes paint every button's text white unless its name appears on an exclusion list — the list exists because a transparent text button needs to opt out, or it is white on a white surface. Six controls had never been added: the hidden-conversations toggle, both Reveal buttons, the Desktop-only filter, and the two new sound controls. Three of them had been invisible on light themes since well before this release.

The rule's own comment had warned about exactly this failure, and the list was still not extended — three separate times. So it is no longer something anyone has to remember: a check now derives the list from the stylesheet itself, and any new transparent control that is missing from it fails before it ships.

## Conscious Exclusions

- **A real failure has never been heard.** The failure tone is covered by tests, including the abandoned-settlement path, but making a settlement genuinely fail is not something that can be arranged on request. It is claimed as tested, not as verified.
- **Changing the sound setting in the middle of a long turn** is handled in the code and is checked there, but was not staged against an actual ninety-second turn.
- **No system notification, no badge, no dock bounce.** The sound reports on something that already happened; it never becomes a second opinion about whether it happened.
- **The light-theme repair is broad and was verified by eye, not by measurement.** Roughly 102 instructions that had been doing nothing now take effect. Each was written by someone who meant it to work, so the expected outcome is the design as intended — but no test can see a colour, and the confirmation is the field's.
- **A settlement still moves the entire conversation**, as it did in the previous release. Still measured, still not reduced.

## What We Learned

**A measurement that returns nothing is worthless until the instrument has been shown capable of returning something.** This release produced three false negatives in a row, each caught only because a control ran *beside* the measurement rather than after someone doubted it: a search tool that read part of its own input as options and reported every colour missing; a check that scanned its own assertions and concluded a missing definition was present; and a pattern that reported the last item of a list absent while the same output counted it.

**And the more expensive version of the same mistake: finding a real defect and stopping there.** The undefined colours were a genuine, 102-rule problem, and fixing them repaired the dark themes. They were never the cause of what had actually been reported. The evidence was in the very first screenshot — one line of text readable, the button beside it white, both asking for the same colour. That asymmetry was the question, and it took another round to ask it.

## Next Horizon

Reduce what a settlement moves, so its cost grows with what a turn added rather than with how long the conversation has become.

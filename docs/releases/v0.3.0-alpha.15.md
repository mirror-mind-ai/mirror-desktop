# v0.3.0-alpha.15 — Noticed While Open

**Date:** 2026-11-04

## Highlights

- **A new version shows up without closing the app.** The version chip asks the update channel
  again every four hours, and when the window comes back after more than an hour.
- **Nothing else changes in how updating works.** The notice is the same chip, installing is still
  your choice, and it still waits for the turn to finish.
- **Still Intel only, and not signed.**

## Where We Started

The update channel was asked once, when the window loaded. The app is used continuously and rarely
closed, so a release published later could go unnoticed for days (CR191).

## What Changed

- **CR191.** While the app is open, the chip looks at the clock every five minutes and asks the
  channel when four hours have passed since the last check, or when the window regains focus or
  becomes visible after more than an hour. A Mac that slept through its timers is covered by the
  return to the window.
- **One check at a time, and silent.** A later check shows no "checking" state, and a failed one
  (offline, channel down) shows nothing; the next moment asks again.
- **The chip keeps what the person is looking at.** A later check never interrupts an installation
  or an error message being read, keeps an update already shown, and moves a shown notice to a
  newer version when one appears.

## What Does Not Change

The same Mirror core and the same Pi as v0.3.0-alpha.14. Nothing in a home's database or in the
app's preferences changes. The update channel and its manifests are unchanged. Mirror Desktop 0.2
is not offered.

## Conscious Exclusions

- **No system notification.** The notice stays in the chip; a notification outside the app was not
  asked for.
- **The intervals are not a setting.** Four hours and one hour were agreed with the Navigator.
- **No signing, notarization or Apple Silicon build** yet.

## What We Learned

The check at load assumed an app that is opened and closed like a document. A workspace that stays
open for days needs its own clock, and a sleeping Mac means the clock alone is not enough: coming
back to the window is the moment that matters.

## Next Horizon

One more release, published while this one stays open, to see the notice arrive without a
restart; then CV-013, the rename to Nautilus for Mirror.

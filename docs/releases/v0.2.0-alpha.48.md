# v0.2.0-alpha.48 — The App Stays Yours While a Turn Finishes

**Date:** 2026-10-13

## Highlights

- **Typing works while a turn is finishing.** Press a key and the letter appears, instead of arriving several seconds later or waiting for `Finishing` to clear and then dumping everything at once.
- **A turn finishing in one Journey no longer freezes the others.** It used to freeze all of them, because they all share one thread.
- **The whole window is responsive, not just the message box** — scrolling, clicking, switching conversations.
- **A turn still takes exactly as long to settle as it did before.** That is deliberate and measured: the freeze was never about how long settling took.
- **Four other performance repairs ship alongside this**, each a real defect, none of them the cause of the freeze.
- **Nothing about what a turn does changed.** Seven turns were run through the new build and every one settled completely, with no loss and no new failure.

## Where We Started

The report was specific: *"when an agent goes into Finishing, writing in the composer gets slow."* The first four attempts at this were all wrong, and they were wrong in the same way, so that is worth saying plainly before describing what actually fixed it.

Each attempt measured something real, repaired it, and shipped. The clock that ticked once a second at the root of the app. The text scans that re-ran over every message on every redraw. A strip operation computed twice when once would do. The message box that waited for a redraw before showing a character. **All four were genuine defects and all four are in this release.** None of them was the cause.

What broke the pattern was three sentences in the fifth report: *the whole app* freezes, not just the composer; the letters **arrive all at once** when `Finishing` ends; and a turn finishing in **one** Journey freezes **all** the others. Each of those was incompatible with the explanation being tried, and the third was decisive — a conversation you are not looking at is not even on screen, so nothing about drawing it could slow anything down.

**The error was not in any of the four repairs. It was in the instrument.** Everything had been measured with the browser profiler, which can see the part of the app that draws, and cannot see the part that reads and writes your files. Four repairs were aimed at the only place the instrument could look.

Measured in the right place, the answer took one minute. macOS gives an application a single thread that delivers every keystroke, click and scroll to every window. Settling a turn — writing the conversation, advancing the journal, delivering to Mirror — was running **on that thread**. One settlement occupied it for just under six seconds, in unbroken blocks of up to 1.6 seconds each. While it was busy, the system had nowhere to deliver a keypress, so keypresses queued in the operating system and were handed over in a burst when the thread came back. That is the freeze, that is why it crossed Journeys, and that is why typed letters appeared all at once.

## What Changed

### The work that settles a turn moved off the thread that serves the window

Sixty-nine of the ninety internal operations that read or write your data now run on background threads. The remaining twenty-one are either already in the background or are things that genuinely belong on the main thread — file pickers, opening a link in your browser, revealing a file in Finder, quitting.

The settling itself is not faster, and that is the point. It still takes about six seconds on a large conversation, the same as before, because it is doing the same work. **It just no longer does it in the place where your typing has to happen.**

### Operations still take turns, now on purpose instead of by accident

Running everything on one thread had an invisible side effect: no two of those operations could ever overlap, because there was only one thread to run them on. Moving them to background threads would have removed that guarantee, and nothing in the storage layer had ever been asked to cope with two writes at once.

Rather than inspect ninety operations one by one and hope the inspection was thorough, the guarantee is now explicit: the operations hold a single shared turnstile, so exactly one runs at a time, exactly as before. **The change is where the work happens, not how it is ordered.**

That decision paid for itself immediately. It surfaced a case where two operations call a third, which would have had all three waiting on each other forever — found before the code was written, because the rule is checked automatically rather than reviewed by eye.

### The four repairs that were not the cause

They ship because they are real, not because they fixed this:

- The app no longer keeps a clock running at its root during `Finishing`.
- Text scans refuse before doing any work when the text cannot contain what they are looking for — measured at an 88% reduction on a large message.
- A redundant text pass was removed by using a value the code had already computed.
- The message box writes your character directly instead of waiting for the app to redraw.
- The conversation view now genuinely skips redrawing when nothing in it changed. It had been *written* to skip since an earlier release, but one line defeated it on every redraw — so it had never skipped once.

## Conscious Exclusions

- **How much a settlement writes has not changed.** On a large conversation, settling still sends the entire conversation across the app's internal boundary three times and reads it back once, which accounts for about a quarter of the time. It now costs a background thread instead of your typing, so you will not feel it — but it is still waste, and it is written down as the next thing rather than quietly tolerated.
- **The offer to stop waiting on a stuck `Finishing` still needs sixty seconds to appear.** No settlement in two hundred and forty recorded ones comes close to that now, so it stays untested in real use and the threshold itself is being reconsidered separately.
- **The one measurement that would show exactly how much of the main thread was freed was not taken**, because the app had already been closed when the result came in. The cause is established; the size of the improvement is inferred from the outcome rather than measured after the fact.

## Lessons

**When repeated repairs at a measured cause do not move the symptom, suspect what the instrument can see before trusting the next explanation.** Four repairs went out against four measurements, all of them accurate about the part of the app they could observe. The report had been describing something outside that part from the beginning — "the whole app", "all the other Journeys", "all at once" — and each of those phrases contradicted the explanation in hand. Changing instrument was worth more than any fifth hypothesis would have been.

**And a fix that does not improve the number can still be the right fix.** Settling takes the same six seconds it always took. If this release had also made it faster, it would be impossible to say which change removed the freeze. It did not, and that is the cleanest possible evidence that the diagnosis was right: the duration was never the symptom.

## Next Horizon

Reduce what a settlement actually moves, so its cost grows with what the turn added rather than with how long the conversation has become.

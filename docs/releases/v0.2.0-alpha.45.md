# v0.2.0-alpha.45 — Two Ways Out

**Date:** 2026-10-11

## Highlights

- **A turn that already finished says so.** When the answer is saved but the screen still reads *Working…*, a notice tells you the turn has finished and nothing was lost — instead of leaving you watching a spinner for a turn that ended minutes ago.
- **A conversation whose process has ended can be released.** If Pi exits without handing its turn back, the app now asks the operating system whether the process is really gone, and offers to finish the turn properly. No restart.
- **The app measures instead of guessing.** The offer appears only when the operating system reports the process has exited — not after a timer, and not because something looks slow. A process that is still running is left alone.

## Where We Started

The previous release let one Journey work in two conversations at once. Running two turns side by side made a failure visible that had been there much longer, and it has a particular cruelty: **everything was working correctly, and you were still stuck.**

A Pi process finished a long answer — all 65,474 characters of it — and exited. Forty-five minutes later the conversation still read as working. The answer was on disk. The process was gone. And every part of the app was behaving exactly as designed:

- Send was unavailable, because the registry said a turn was running.
- *Stop waiting* did not appear, because it refuses to offer an exit while a process may really be executing.
- The repair routine declined, for the same reason.
- The conversation's mark read `WORKING`, because it reads the same source.

**Each of those refusals was correct, and all of them were reading one stale fact.** The only exit was quitting the app — and the record of that turn never healed. It had happened at least three times, twice in September, and nobody had seen it, because each occurrence just looked like a slow turn you eventually restarted past.

Two surfaces disagreeing on one screen is what made it findable: a Journey header reading `FINISHING` beside a conversation mark reading `WORKING`. The marks shipped two releases ago turned out to be the instrument that measured the bug.

## What Changed

### A finished turn stops pretending to work

The renderer now has a way to ask the durable record whether a turn it believes is streaming has in fact already finished. When the record says the turn settled and the screen still says otherwise, you get a notice: *This turn has already finished*, the answer is saved, nothing is lost.

It is deliberately quiet about the opposite case. Telling you a working turn has finished would be worse than the original defect, so the check only speaks when the record is unambiguous, and it waits a few seconds past the point where a healthy turn's bookkeeping is briefly in two states at once.

### A conversation whose Pi has exited can be released

The app can now ask the operating system a direct question — is this process still alive? — and the answer travels with the rest of the conversation's status. When a turn's process has exited but the turn was never handed back, you are offered *Release this turn*.

Taking it runs the same finish every normal turn runs. Your message and the answer stay exactly as they are. Before anything is released, the app checks again that the process has ended; one that is still running is refused, not killed. And nothing happening in another conversation is touched.

### Everything else stayed where it was

The refusals described above were not loosened. Send still refuses while a turn is really running, *Stop waiting* still withholds itself while a process may be executing, and the repair routine still declines. **What changed is the fact they read, not their caution.** Once a dead turn is released, those four surfaces work exactly as they were designed to — including *Stop waiting*, which becomes reachable for the first time in the situation it was written for.

## Conscious Exclusions

- **Why the hand-back fails in the first place is still unknown.** This release makes the consequence recoverable. It does not explain the cause, and does not claim to.
- **A turn already stranded before this release stays stranded in the record.** Its entry can be released going forward, but the durable record of the three historical occurrences could only be cleaned up by hand, with the app closed.
- **The release is offered, never automatic.** The app measures; you decide. An app that silently reclaimed turns it believed were dead would be a worse failure than the one being fixed.
- **A turn that is genuinely hung — running but making no progress — still has no exit.** That is a different defect and it is captured separately.
- **A recovered turn is still stored twice in its conversation file.** The screen is correct; the stored record is not. Captured separately, and deliberately not bundled in here.

## What We Learned

**A guard that defers to a possibly-stale authority has the same failure shape as a wrong guard.** *Stop waiting* was written for exactly this situation and was disabled by exactly this situation. The lesson is not that the guard was too strict — it was right — but that a refusal is only as good as the fact it reads.

**Correct, together, can still be insufficient.** Four independent components each did the right thing with the same false premise. Reviewing any one of them would have found nothing wrong.

**Measurement beats patience.** The first instinct was to offer the exit after a long enough wait. Elapsed time cannot distinguish a dead process from a slow one. Asking the operating system can.

**The plan was wrong twice, and the code said so both times.** The first approach would have given a different label and the same cage. The second looked free because the release machinery already existed — until it turned out that machinery refuses the exact state this defect produces. Both corrections are on the record where the plan was written, not quietly replaced.

**Some correct behaviour cannot be proven in the field.** The release offer appears only when the defect recurs, which cannot be staged on demand. It ships tested rather than field-verified, and that distinction is written down rather than rounded up.

## Next Horizon

Why a finished Pi fails to hand its turn back remains the open question, and it is the one worth answering. Beneath it sits a smaller one with real cost: a turn record that outlives its process currently occupies its slot forever, because the rule that protects live work from being pruned also protects dead work.

Returning to the conversation you left — named as the next step two releases ago — is still waiting, still independent, and still cheap.
